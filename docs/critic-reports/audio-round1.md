# Critic report — audio, round 1

Score: **8.3 / 10** — Pass: **no** (threshold 8.5; console errors 0; contract violations 0; rule violations 0)

Verdict in one line: the engine is contract-complete and its timing is exact — every scheduled note sits on the tempo grid to the sample, seek and tempo storms produce zero duplicates or hanging notes, pause and seek are click-free in a live capture, end-of-score and restart behave — but it is not yet something I would put in front of paying users: the scheduler dies in a throttled tab, a failed `resume()` leaves the transport "playing" with a frozen clock and no way back through `play()`, seeking into a held note drops that voice, and the mixer meters lie (red at −20 dB). ## What is good

- Timing is exact, not approximately right: 0.000 ms grid error on every logged onset, tempo re-basing keeps the playhead to 0.005 qn, and the storms (25 seeks in 300 ms; 122 tempo changes in 1.2 s; 12 play/pause toggles at 55 ms) leave no duplicates, no gaps and no orphaned voices. This is the hard part of a lookahead scheduler and it is done properly (`ClockAnchor` + `rebase` + cursor keyed voices).
- Click-free by construction and by measurement: `cancelAndHoldAtTime` + `setTargetAtTime` on release, analytic envelope continuity at note end, verified in both offline and live capture.
- Mixer semantics are right (mute > solo > fader), smoothed on the node, reflected synchronously, and `renderOffline` follows them.
- Robust inputs: NaN/±∞/out-of-range tempo, seek, gains and unknown track ids never throw; empty scores and concurrent `play()` are handled.
- The showcase is a genuinely useful instrument: piano-roll that fills only as notes hit the audio clock (so a seek leaves a visible hole), live scope and log spectrum, per-track dB meters, a scheduled-notes table with context time and durations, offline mix-vs-solo waveforms with RMS, and a piano-vs-strings comparison with spectral centroids and correlation. Diagnostics carry per-step snapshots and the contract-method list.
- Honest builder report: every gap I found in the code was already in `knownGaps`, and the fps attribution turned out to be correct.

## What is wrong (ranked)

1. **[major] The scheduler collapses under timer throttling** — `engine.ts:242` drives the 150 ms lookahead from `window.setInterval(tick, 25)`. Simulating a 1 s timer (what Chrome and Firefox do to hidden tabs; Chrome exempts a page only while it is actually audible, so every rest longer than ~2 s re-arms the throttle) made 5 of 13 notes start 228–725 ms late and shortened them (`probe2.json → throttledTimer`). A user who starts playback and switches tabs hears notes clumped onto the tick. Fix: run the tick in a `Worker` (or `AudioWorklet` clock), or at minimum widen the horizon to ≥ 1.2 s when `document.visibilityState === 'hidden'` and tick immediately on `visibilitychange`.
2. **[major] A failed or slow `resume()` leaves the engine "playing" with a frozen clock, and `play()` cannot recover it** — `resumeWithTimeout` (`engine.ts:46-55, 317`) resolves after 1.5 s regardless; the engine then sets `playing = true` on a suspended context. Probe: `playing true, contextState 'suspended', positionQn 0` for 500 ms; a second `play()` returned immediately as a no-op because `playing` is already true, so the UI's toggle would call `pause()` instead. This is exactly the state an iOS interruption or a blocked autoplay produces: a stuck Pause button and silence. Fix: only enter `playing` when `ctx.state === 'running'` (otherwise reject/resolve with `playing false` and notify), and in `play()` treat `playing && ctx.state !== 'running'` as "retry resume"; re-anchor on `statechange` to running.
3. **[major] Seeking into a held note (or rendering offline from inside one) drops that voice until its next onset** — `resetCursors` / `cursorAt` (`engine.ts:229-231, 429`) start only notes with `startQn >= position`. Probe: `seek(0.5)` then play → the qn-0 left-hand chord (2 qn long) never sounds, only qn 1 (`probe1.json → seekPaused.heldChordScheduledWhenSeekingIntoIt: false`); `renderOffline({fromQn: 21})` likewise omits anything sounding across 21. Real repertoire is full of long bass notes and ties across barlines, so a seek-slider drop or measure click regularly starts with the accompaniment missing for a beat or more. Fix: on play/seek/offline start, also start notes with `startQn < pos < startQn + durationQn` for their remaining duration (a fresh attack is acceptable; the analytic `envelopeValueAt` already exists if you want to join the decay mid-way).
4. **[minor] Mixer meter shows red at −20 dB** — `.meter-fill` (`showcase.css`) carries the danger/warn/ok gradient and is scaled with `transform: scaleY()`, so the gradient scales with the level and the top of the fill is always red (visible in 02–06: red tips at −14 to −27 dB). A meter that is red at −20 dB is misleading to anyone who reads meters. Fix: paint the gradient on the meter track and reveal it (clip-path/inset cover from the top) instead of scaling the coloured element.
5. **[minor] A tempo change leaves already-sounding notes at their old-tempo duration** — `setTempo` (`engine.ts:354-367`) re-times only pending voices, so at 60 → 300 bpm a sounding whole note (4 s) rings over the next several notes; on the sustained timbre this is a smear, on piano a muddle. Fix: for sounding voices, re-schedule the release at `timeForQn(newAnchor, startQn + durationQn)` (cancelAndHold at now, then setTargetAtTime at the new end).
6. **[minor] Output is quiet and mono** — mix RMS −23.8 dBFS / peak −10.3 dBFS at velocity 0.8 with `peakMax 0.5` and master 0.9, and L = R sample-for-sample. Users will reach for the system volume, and a "layered, mixable" player benefits from even a slight per-track pan (e.g. staff index spread ±0.2). The compressor is already there to catch peaks; consider raising `velocityToPeak` ceiling or the default master, and a `StereoPannerNode` per track.
7. **[minor] `setTrackGain(id, NaN)` silences the track** — `clampGain(NaN)` returns 0 (`mix.ts:3-6`), so a NaN from a UI control mutes rather than being ignored (probe: `tgAfterNaN: 0`). Match `setTempo`/`seek` and ignore non-finite input.
8. **[minor] Sustained timbre swells on same-pitch retriggers** — 200 ms release + 60 ms attack overlap (`envelope.ts:102-120`); screenshot 09 shows the repeated G4 at 2.5 s at ~2–3× the previous G4. Fix: on a retrigger of the same pitch in the same track, release the previous voice fast (35 ms) or steal it.
9. **[minor] `engine.ts` and `synth.ts` have no automated tests** — the 41 unit tests cover only the pure helpers; all engine-level guarantees (duplicates, end-of-score, concurrency, resume failure) rest on ad-hoc puppeteer probes that are not in the repo. A vitest suite with a minimal fake `BaseAudioContext` (or `standardized-audio-context`-style mock) for `engine.ts` would catch regressions in items 2 and 3 cheaply.
10. **[minor] Process nit** — do not touch `tools/verify/**` even to delete a stale screenshot; it is the integrator's folder and the output dir is gitignored anyway.

Not counted against the module: the 31 fps figure (environmental, proven with a same-minute control on the ui showcase); the log-gain column reading a constant 0.250 (all demo velocities are 0.8; the pending change request pins the unit); the ~170 ms audible fade on pause (smooth, musical, the compressor's doing); the missing 8th onset in the verifier heuristic (masked by the held chord — a heuristic limit, not a synthesis fault).

## Score

8.3 / 10. Contract-complete, timing-exact, click-free and honestly reported — a very good engine core. Three real gaps stand between it and "ship it": a scheduler that depends on an unthrottled main-thread timer, a transport state machine that can get stuck "playing" on a suspended context, and no note-chasing on seek. Fix those three and the meter gradient and this passes comfortably.
